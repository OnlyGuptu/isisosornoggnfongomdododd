1. A merchant in the port city of Lothal is exchanging bags of spices for copper ingots. He receives 15 ingots for every 2 bags of spices. If he brings 12 bags of spices to the market, how many copper ingots will he leave with?

  • Rate of exchange: 2 bags of spices = 15 copper ingots.
  • Number of sets of 2 bags in 12 bags: 12 / 2 = 6 sets.
  • Total copper ingots received: 6 × 15 = 90 copper ingots.

2. Look at the sequence of numbers on one column of the Ishango bone: 11, 13, 17, 19. What do these numbers have in common? List the next three numbers that fit this pattern.

  • Common Property: They are all consecutive prime numbers (numbers greater than 1 with no positive divisors other than 1 and themselves). Specifically, they are the prime numbers between 10 and 20.
  • Next Three Numbers: The next three prime numbers following 19 are 23, 29, and 31.
Q3

Exercise Set 3.1

3. We know that Natural Numbers are closed under addition (the sum of any two natural numbers is always a natural number). Are they closed under subtraction? Provide a couple of examples to justify your answer.

No, natural numbers are not closed under subtraction.

  • Subtracting a larger natural number from a smaller one or subtracting a number from itself does not yield a natural number:
    • 3 - 5 = -2 (which is a negative integer, not a natural number).
    • 4 - 4 = 0 (zero is a whole number/integer, not a natural number).

Exercise Set 3.1

4. Ancient Indians used the joints of their fingers to count, a practice still seen today. Each finger has 3 joints, and the thumb is used to count them. How many can you count on one hand? How does this relate to the ancient base-12 counting systems?

  • Count on one hand: The four fingers (index, middle, ring, pinky) each have 3 distinct segments/joints. Using the thumb as a pointer gives:
    4 fingers × 3 joints = 12 distinct counts on a single hand.
  • Relation to base-12 system: This physiological method is the natural foundation for duodecimal (base-12) systems. It explains the historical division of days and nights into 12 hours each (total 24 hours), the 12 signs of the zodiac, and units like dozens (12) and grosses (144).

Exercise Set 3.2

4. Explain, using a real-world example of debt, why subtracting a negative number is the same as adding a positive number (e.g., 10 - (-5) = 15).

  • Suppose you have ₹10 in cash, but you also hold a formal debt (negative value) of ₹5 owed to a storekeeper.
  • If someone generously waives or removes that ₹5 debt for you, they are subtracting a debt of 5: -(-5).
  • Relieving you of that ₹5 obligation increases your financial net worth by ₹5. Therefore, removing a ₹5 debt is identical to being handed ₹5 in cash:
    10 - (-5) = 10 + 5 = 15.

1. Prove that the following rational numbers are equal:

(i) 2/3 and 4/6      (ii) 5/4 and 10/8      (iii) -3/5 and -6/10      (iv) 9/3 and 3

Two rational numbers a/b and c/d are equal if their cross-products satisfy ad = bc:

  • (i) 2/3 and 4/6: 2 × 6 = 12 and 3 × 4 = 12. Since 12 = 12, 2/3 = 4/6.
  • (ii) 5/4 and 10/8: 5 × 8 = 40 and 4 × 10 = 40. Since 40 = 40, 5/4 = 10/8.
  • (iii) -3/5 and -6/10: (-3) × 10 = -30 and 5 × (-6) = -30. Since -30 = -30, -3/5 = -6/10.
  • (iv) 9/3 and 3/1: 9 × 1 = 9 and 3 × 3 = 9. Since 9 = 9, 9/3 = 3.

6. Can you think of other way(s) to find a rational number between any two rational numbers?

  • Method of Equivalent Fractions: Convert both rational numbers to equivalent fractions with a much larger common denominator, then select any intermediate integer numerator (e.g., between 1/3 = 10/30 and 2/3 = 20/30, choose 11/30, 12/30, etc.).

Q4

Exercise Set 3.5

4. The number 0.9̅ (which means 0.99999...) is a rational number. Using algebra (let x = 0.9̅, multiply by 10, and subtract), explain why 0.9̅ is exactly equal to 1.

  1. Let x = 0.99999... — (Equation 1)
  2. Multiply both sides by 10:
    10x = 9.99999... — (Equation 2)
  3. Subtract Equation 1 from Equation 2:
    10x - x = 9.99999... - 0.99999...
    9x = 9
  4. Divide both sides by 9:
    x = 9/9 = 1

Thus, 0.9̅ is exactly equal to 1. This demonstrates the non-uniqueness of decimal representations for rational numbers.

Q2

End-of-Chapter Exercises

2. Prove that √5 is an irrational number.

Proof by Contradiction:

  1. Assume to the contrary that √5 is a rational number.
  2. Therefore, it can be expressed in simplest form as √5 = p/q, where p and q are non-zero co-prime integers (sharing no common factor other than 1).
  3. Squaring both sides gives: 5 = p² / q² ⇒ p² = 5q².
  4. This means p² is divisible by 5, which implies that p must also be divisible by 5 (since 5 is a prime number). Let p = 5k for some integer k.
  5. Substitute p = 5k back into the equation:
    (5k)² = 5q² ⇒ 25k² = 5q² ⇒ q² = 5k².
  6. This indicates that q² is divisible by 5, which means q must also be divisible by 5.
  7. Hence, both p and q have at least 5 as a common factor. This directly contradicts our initial statement that p and q are co-prime.
  8. Therefore, our assumption is false, and √5 is an irrational number.

10. A rational number has a terminating decimal expansion whose last non-zero digit occurs in the 4th decimal place. Show that such a number can be written in the form p/10⁴ where p is an integer not divisible by 10. Is it necessary that the denominator of this rational number, when written in the lowest form, is divisible by 2⁴ or 5⁴? Give reasons.

  • Representation: A number with 4 decimal places can be written as p / 10⁴. If p were divisible by 10, the final 0 would cancel out with 10⁴, leaving fewer than 4 decimal places. Thus, p is not divisible by 10.
  • Divisibility by 2⁴ or 5⁴: Yes. In lowest terms, p / (2⁴ × 5⁴) cannot cancel both prime factors 2 and 5 simultaneously because 10 does not divide p. Therefore, at least one of the prime factor powers 2⁴ or 5⁴ must remain fully intact in the denominator.

11. Without performing division, determine whether the decimal expansion of 18/125 is terminating or non-terminating. If it terminates, state the number of decimal places.

  • Fraction in lowest terms: 18/125 (18 and 125 share no common factors).
  • Prime factorization of denominator: 125 = 5³ = 2° × 5³.
  • Since the denominator contains only powers of 5, the decimal expansion is terminating.
  • The highest power between 2 and 5 is 3 (5³), so it terminates after 3 decimal places (18/125 = 144/1000 = 0.144).

12. A rational number in its lowest form has denominator 2³ × 5. How many decimal places will its decimal expansion have? Explain your answer.

Explain why the condition k₂ - k₁ > n + 1 is necessary to find n such rational numbers between the two rational numbers a and b using this method.

  • Equalizing Denominators:

    a = 7/12 = 14/24 (k₁ = 14)
    b = 5/6 = 20/24 (k₂ = 20)

Q14

End-of-Chapter Exercises

14. Three rational numbers x, y, z satisfy x + y + z = 0 and xy + yz + zx = 0. Show that all the rational numbers x, y, z must be simultaneously zero.

Proof:

  1. Consider the algebraic identity:
    (x + y + z)² = x² + y² + z² + 2(xy + yz + zx)
  2. Substitute the given values x + y + z = 0 and xy + yz + zx = 0:
    0² = x² + y² + z² + 2(0)
    x² + y² + z² = 0
  3. Since x, y, z are rational numbers, their squares x², y², z² must all be non-negative real values (≥ 0).
  4. The sum of non-negative terms can only equal 0 if each individual term is 0:
    x² = 0 ⇒ x = 0
    y² = 0 ⇒ y = 0
    z² = 0 ⇒ z = 0
  5. Thus, x, y, and z must be simultaneously zero.
Q15

End-of-Chapter Exercises

15. Show that the rational number (a + b)/2 lies between the rational numbers a and b.

Let a < b:

  1. Add a to both sides of the inequality:
    a + a < a + b ⇒ 2a < a + b ⇒ a < (a + b)/2
  2. Add b to both sides of the original inequality:
    a + b < b + b ⇒ a + b < 2b ⇒ (a + b)/2 < b
  3. Combining both results gives:
    a < (a + b)/2 < b

Hence, the average (a + b)/2 always lies strictly between a and b.

At NCERTSolved (accessible via https://ncertsolved.in), the privacy of our visitors is of paramount importance to us. This Privacy Policy document outlines the types of personal information collected, recorded, and utilized by NCERTSolved.

1. Log Files & Web Analytics

NCERTSolved follows a standard procedure of using server log files. These files log visitors when they visit websites. The information collected by log files includes internet protocol (IP) addresses, browser type, Internet Service Provider (ISP), date/time stamps, referring/exit pages, and number of clicks. These are not linked to any information that is personally identifiable. The purpose of this information is for analyzing trends, administering the site, tracking users' movement around the website, and gathering demographic information to ensure high performance and reliability.

2. Cookies and Web Beacons

Like any other website, NCERTSolved uses 'cookies' to store information including visitors' preferences and the pages on the website that the visitor accessed or visited. The information is used to optimize the users' experience by customizing our web page content based on visitors' browser type and/or other information.

3. Google AdSense & DoubleClick DART Cookies

Google is one of our third-party advertising partners. Google uses cookies, known as DART cookies, to serve advertisements to our site visitors based upon their visit to ncertsolved.in and other websites on the internet:

  • Third-party vendors, including Google, use cookies to serve ads based on a user's prior visits to your website or other websites.
  • Google's use of advertising cookies enables it and its partners to serve ads to users based on their visit to your sites and/or other sites on the Internet.
  • Users may opt out of personalized advertising by visiting Google Ads Settings.
  • Alternatively, visitors can opt out of third-party vendor use of cookies for personalized advertising by visiting www.aboutads.info/choices/.

4. California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) Rights

Under the CCPA, California consumers have the right to:

  • Request that a business that collects personal data disclose the categories and specific pieces of personal data collected about consumers.
  • Request that a business delete any personal data about the consumer that a business collected.
  • Request that a business that sells personal data not sell the consumer's personal data. (NCERTSolved does not sell personal data).

5. General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) Rights

Every user from the European Economic Area (EEA) is entitled to the right to access, the right to rectification, the right to erasure, the right to restrict processing, the right to object to processing, and the right to data portability. If you make a request, we have one month to respond to you.

6. Children's Online Privacy Protection (COPPA)

NCERTSolved prioritizes the protection of children using the internet. We encourage parents and guardians to observe, participate in, and/or monitor their online activity. NCERTSolved does not knowingly collect any Personal Identifiable Information from children under the age of 13. If you believe your child provided this kind of information on our website, please contact us immediately and we will promptly remove such records.

7. Contact Information

If you have additional questions or require more information about our Privacy Policy, do not hesitate to contact our compliance team at contact.ncertsolved@gmail.com.

About NCERTSolved

Student-First Pedagogical Hub • Editorial Standards • Academic Integrity

1. Our Educational Mission

NCERTSolved was founded by students and educators with a singular goal: to provide high-school students with crystal-clear, verified, and accessible textbook solutions. Traditional educational portals often conceal answers behind intrusive paywalls, popups, or low-quality automated scans. We deliver clean, step-by-step textbook solutions accompanied by interactive mathematical formulas, labelled diagrams, and conceptual hints designed to build genuine conceptual mastery.

2. Rigorous Editorial & Verification Methodology (E-E-A-T)

Every single solution published on NCERTSolved undergoes a multi-tier academic review process:

  • Formula & Proof Verification: Mathematical solutions (Ganita Prakash / Ganita Manjari) are verified step-by-step using both algebraic proof methods and Baudhāyana-Pythagorean geometric verification.
  • Scientific Accuracy: Science solutions (Physics, Chemistry, and Biology) are vetted against official CBSE/NCERT curriculum benchmarks to ensure proper SI units, balanced chemical equations, and accurate biological nomenclature.
  • Original Pedagogical Hints: Rather than mere answer keys, exercises feature dedicated "💡 Show Hint / Formula" callouts to foster problem-solving intuition.
  • Human Review: Content is created, reviewed, and updated by human educators and senior student peer tutors.

3. Fair Use & Non-Affiliation Disclaimer

NCERT (National Council of Educational Research and Training) and CBSE (Central Board of Secondary Education) are registered statutory bodies and autonomous organizations of the Government of India.
