🌍 Chapter 3: Resistance and Resilience (1000 CE - 1700 CE)

Verified step-by-step solutions for your class notes.

Q1

Questions and Activities

1. Choose the most appropriate answer.
Resistance and resilience are closely connected because:
a. Resistance always involves warfare.
b. Resilience enables communities to endure difficulties and sustain resistance.
c. Resistance is possible only when a kingdom has a large army.
d. Resilient societies avoid all forms of conflict.

Correct Answer: b. Resilience enables communities to endure difficulties and sustain resistance.

While resistance involves actively opposing a threat, the capacity to absorb hardship and adapt over time comes from resilience. Therefore, resilience serves as the backbone that allows communities to sustain their resistance.

Q2

Questions and Activities

2. Why and how did groups such as the Sikhs, Marathas, and Rajputs assert their political power and identity during periods of changing political authority?

These groups asserted their political power to safeguard their sovereignty, territories, and socio-cultural traditions from centralized empires like the Delhi Sultanate and the Mughals.

  • Rajputs: They utilized rugged terrains (like the Aravalli hills) for guerrilla warfare, built strong forts, and formed strategic alliances or confederacies to maintain regional autonomy.
  • Marathas: Driven by the concept of swarajya (self-rule), they built extensive networks of hill forts, employed swift guerrilla tactics, and established tributary relationships to exhaust the Mughal Empire.
  • Sikhs: Facing severe religious persecution and the execution of their Gurus, they combined spiritual and political identity (miri-piri), formed the Khalsa with a distinct martial code, and organized into armed confederacies (misals) to defend their faith and land.
Q3

Questions and Activities

3. Why did some Rajput rulers decide to negotiate alliances with the Mughal power?

Some Rajput rulers, such as the Kachhwahas of Amber, decided to negotiate cooperative alliances with the Mughals based on strategic considerations. The Mughals needed the Rajputs' military strength and administrative loyalty to consolidate their vast empire. In return, integrating into the Mughal system through grants of mansabs (ranks) provided these Rajput rulers with political stability, wealth, and continued local authority without the devastation of constant warfare.

Q4

Questions and Activities

4. Imagine a market in a port town of India such as Surat, Masulipatnam, or Puducherry. You are a foreign trader from Italy or France and want to make a deal of cotton textiles. How would you secure the product in the local market? Whose help would you seek and what kind of challenges would you face in terms of language, quality of product, prices of commodities, etc.?

To secure cotton textiles, I would need to navigate the established local economic systems and supply chains.

  • Whose help to seek: I would seek the assistance of powerful local merchant groups (like Gujarati or Coromandel merchants), brokers, and dubhashis (interpreters/translators) who act as intermediaries between foreign companies and local weavers.
  • Challenges:
    - Language: Overcoming communication barriers would require relying heavily on local dubhashis or merchant groups who learned European languages.
    - Prices and Quality: I would face intense competition from established Arab, Chinese, and other European traders. Ensuring quality and negotiating prices would require using local financial instruments like the hundii and building trust with local bankers (sarrafs).
Q5

Questions and Activities

5. Watch any video at the given link (https://www.indianculture.gov.in/forts-of-india/tales-of-forts) and analyse the various functions of forts and fortresses. How did rulers perceive the importance of forts in terms of strategic concerns.

(Based on the chapter's context regarding forts:)

Rulers perceived forts as essential strategic nuclei for survival and resistance. Forts functioned as:

  • Military Bases: They enabled rulers (like the Marathas and Rajputs) to launch swift guerrilla attacks and provided safe retreats.
  • Administrative and Judicial Centres: Forts housed the administration and authority of the state.
  • Refuges during Sieges: Equipped with strong defenses, armouries, and water sources, forts like Raigad or Senji provided the self-sufficiency needed to withstand prolonged enemy blockades and avoid total defeat.
Q6

Questions and Activities

6. What architectural changes occurred in temple construction during the period under study? How do these changes reflect upon the resilient nature of Indian civilisation?

From the 16th century onwards, temples were often constructed in less visible locations, such as along narrow lanes, with entrances that did not open onto main streets. They lacked tall shikharas and the conventional appearance of temples, instead resembling ordinary residential houses (often referred to as 'haveli temples').

These architectural adaptations reflect the resilient nature of Indian civilization, as communities creatively modified their sacred structures to avoid visibility and destruction by invading forces, ensuring that their cultural and religious practices could continue uninterrupted.

Q7

Questions and Activities

7. A kingdom has limited military resources but possesses fertile agricultural land, strong local support, and detailed knowledge of its terrain. Explain two ways in which these resources could contribute to the kingdom's resilience when faced with a threat.

  1. Tactical Advantage (Terrain Knowledge & Support): Detailed knowledge of forests, river systems, or rugged hills allows a smaller force to avoid direct confrontation and instead use guerrilla warfare, ambushes, and surprise attacks (as successfully demonstrated by the Ahoms in Assam and the Marathas in the Deccan). Strong local support ensures reliable logistics, guides, and safe havens.
  2. Economic Sustenance (Fertile Land): Fertile agricultural land ensures a continuous food supply and surplus revenue, which is critical for sustaining the population and the military during prolonged sieges or extended conflicts.
Q8

Questions and Activities

8. Consider the following statement: "A tradition becomes more resilient when people can carry it with them." Do you agree? Explain using examples from the Bhakti tradition.

Yes, I agree.

The Bhakti tradition exemplifies this perfectly. When physical temples faced restrictions or destruction, devotees shifted to non-material forms of worship that they could "carry with them," such as nam kirtan (recitation of the divine name) and community singing (satsang). By composing hymns in local, accessible languages (like Tulsidas's Ramcharitmanas in Awadhi or the vakhs of Lal Ded), the tradition moved from rigid physical structures into the households and daily lives of the common people. This adaptability made the tradition far more resilient, widespread, and difficult for any external authority to suppress.

Q9

Questions and Activities

9. Voices from the Past: Choose any two of the following and write a short first-person account explaining what resilience means from that person's perspective: (A Bhakti saint, A Rajput soldier, An Ahom commander, A Maratha administrator, A merchant, A Jat leader)

(Representative accounts based on the text:)

  • An Ahom Commander: "For me, resilience is the river and the forest. We may not have the massive armies of the Mughals, but we know the Brahmaputra's currents and the monsoon's fury. By using our fast bachari boats and drawing the enemy into our challenging terrain, we bend like the bamboo in the wind, only to strike back when they are exhausted. Our independence is secured by our patience and our land."
  • A Bhakti Saint: "Resilience is the devotion that lives in the heart, not just in stone temples. When our sacred sites are threatened, we sing the divine name in the streets and in our homes. By writing hymns in the language of the common people, I ensure that our faith and solidarity cannot be crushed by any king's sword. True strength is found in our shared community and love for the divine."
Q10

Questions and Activities

10. Design a Fort. You are a military architect advising a ruler. Draw a simple, labelled plan of a fort that includes the following: Residential complex, Defensive walls, Water source, Food storage, Escape route, Watchtower, Administrative area. Now, write a few sentences explaining how your design would help the kingdom withstand a long siege.

Strategic Fortress Plan: Concentric defense, elevated citadel, self-sufficient water & grain reserves, and concealed evacuation trail.

Design & Siege Defense Strategy:

To withstand a prolonged siege, the fort is built on elevated rocky terrain featuring concentric defensive walls and multiple high watchtowers along the curtain wall to monitor enemy movements and eliminate blind spots.

Deep within the secure inner citadel lies a perennial water source (stepwell/cistern) and a fortified granary / food storage, ensuring the garrison and inhabitants can comfortably survive for months without external provisioning.

The administrative complex and residential quarters are situated centrally for governance and protection. Finally, a concealed secret escape route (sally port) through the rear cliffs ensures leadership can safely evacuate, regroup, and launch counter-offensives if the outer defenses are ever breached.
